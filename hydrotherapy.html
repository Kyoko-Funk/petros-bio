Hydrotherapy

Movement, supported by water

Supervised exercise in warm water. The water carries part of your weight, so you can move more freely, with less load on painful joints.

What is hydrotherapy?

Exercise, made gentler

Hydrotherapy, also called aquatherapy, is biokinetic exercise done in a warm pool. Your programme is planned and supervised in the same way as on land: we assess you first, set goals together and adjust the exercises as you improve.

It is often a good starting point when exercise on land is too painful, or as a step on the way back to it.

Why water helps

What the water does for you

Support

Water carries part of your body weight, which reduces the load on your joints.

Warmth

Warm water can help stiff muscles and joints relax, so moving feels easier.

Gentle resistance

Moving through water builds strength without heavy weights. Move faster for more resistance.

Confidence

Practise balance and walking with the water around you, under supervision.

Who it can help

Often a good fit for

  • Arthritis and joint pain
  • Early recovery after injury
  • Rehabilitation after surgery, once healed
  • Back pain
  • Fibromyalgia and chronic pain
  • Balance and confidence
  • Pregnancy
  • Neurological conditions
  • Anyone who finds exercise on land difficult
Your session

What a session looks like

Every session is planned around you. A typical one follows four calm steps.

  1. 1

    Arrive and check in

    We ask how you are feeling and check your plan for the day.

  2. 2

    Warm up

    Easy walking and movement in the water to loosen up.

  3. 3

    Your exercises

    Strength, mobility and balance exercises, supervised and adjusted as you go.

  4. 4

    Cool down

    Gentle stretching, and a note of what to do before next time.

Is it right for me?

We check together

Your assessment tells us whether hydrotherapy suits you. A few things to know beforehand:

Good to know

  • You don't need to be able to swim. Exercises are done standing or holding on.
  • We go at your pace, including if you are nervous in water.
  • Many people combine water and land sessions.

Please tell us first if you have

  • An open wound, recent surgical scar that hasn't healed, or a skin infection
  • A heart or blood pressure condition that is not well controlled
  • Breathing problems, epilepsy, or problems with bladder or bowel control

This doesn't always rule hydrotherapy out. It helps us plan safely.

What to bring

  • Swimwear and a towel
  • Slip-on shoes or flip-flops
  • A water bottle

Questions

Hydrotherapy FAQ

Do I need to be able to swim?

No. Exercises are done standing in the water or holding on to the side, and you are supervised throughout.

Is it the same as swimming lessons or aqua aerobics?

No. Hydrotherapy is a supervised exercise programme planned around your condition and goals, the same way as your sessions on land.

Can I do hydrotherapy after surgery?

Often, yes, once your wound has fully healed and your surgeon agrees. We follow your surgeon's guidance.

How do I start?

Book an assessment. We will look at how you move, talk about your goals, and decide together whether water, land or both suit you best.

Ready to get in the water?

Book an assessment, or send us a message if you are not sure hydrotherapy is right for you.
